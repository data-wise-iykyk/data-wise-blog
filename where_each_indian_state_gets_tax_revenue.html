Where Each Indian State Gets Its Tax Revenue From

While U.S. states lean heavily on property and income taxes, Indian states cannot tax personal wages—turning consumption, fuel, alcohol, and real estate deals into their financial lifelines.

Published November 2026  ·  Adapted from Visual Capitalist  ·  Data: RBI State Finances & State Budget Estimates

Key Takeaways

  • SGST is the #1 revenue engine across every major Indian state, generating between 35% and 51% of States' Own Tax Revenue (SOTR).
  • Fuel & Liquor fill the non-GST gap: Because petrol, diesel, and alcohol remain outside GST, Sales Tax/VAT and State Excise together supply 30% to 44% of tax collections in states like Tamil Nadu, Telangana, and Uttar Pradesh.
  • Dry states show sharp fiscal trade-offs: Alcohol prohibition leaves Gujarat (0.2%) and Bihar (0.0%) with virtually zero excise income, forcing higher reliance on SGST, fuel VAT, and industrial/vehicle levies.
  • Real estate booms boost stamp duties: Rapidly urbanizing states like Maharashtra (15.8%), Haryana (13.8%), and Karnataka (12.8%) earn massive shares from property registration fees.

Why India’s Tax Mix Looks Nothing Like America’s

In the United States, property taxes are the largest tax source in 25 states (reaching 64% in New Hampshire), while states like California and New York rely heavily on state-level personal income taxes. Under India’s constitutional division of powers, however, state governments cannot levy personal or corporate income taxes, and property taxes are collected locally by municipal corporations (like BBMP or BMC) rather than state treasuries.

Instead, Indian states fund schools, highways, and welfare schemes through States’ Own Tax Revenue (SOTR)—a basket dominated by indirect consumption taxes—alongside their share of central taxes recommended by the Finance Commission.

1. The SGST Consumption Engine

Introduced in 2017, State GST accounts for ~42% of own-tax collections nationally. Consumer-heavy and service-led states like Maharashtra (₹3.54L Cr SOTR) and Karnataka (₹1.90L Cr) capture massive SGST from urban retail, IT, and manufacturing.

2. The "Sin & Fuel" Cushion

Petrol, diesel, and alcohol were deliberately kept out of GST so states could retain fiscal autonomy. Tamil Nadu (33.4% VAT) and Kerala (32.8% VAT) rely heavily on fuel and liquor sales taxes, while UP (21.8%) and Chhattisgarh (22.4%) lead in state excise.

3. The Urban Real Estate Multiplier

Every apartment purchase or land transfer triggers state stamp duty and registration charges. Booming housing markets in Mumbai-Pune, Bengaluru, and Gurugram-Noida push stamp duties above 12%–15% of state tax revenue.

The Outliers: Dry States and Resource Hubs

Just as Delaware (corporate franchise taxes) and North Dakota (oil severance taxes) stand out in the U.S., policy choices create sharp outliers across India. Gujarat and Bihar enforce alcohol prohibition, forfeiting thousands of crores in annual excise revenue. Gujarat offsets this through its massive industrial SGST base (46.8%) and high fuel VAT (24.5%), whereas Bihar leans on SGST (49.4%) and central tax devolution.

Meanwhile, mineral-rich states like Odisha and Jharkhand supplement their tax base with heavy non-tax mining royalties on iron ore and coal, reducing their dependence on urban stamp duties (under 5.5%).
