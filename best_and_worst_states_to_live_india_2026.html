Adapting Visual Capitalist’s 50-state livability framework to India—weighing economic opportunity, healthcare & education, infrastructure, and public safety across 25 major states.

Published November 2026 · Sources: NITI Aayog SDG India Index, RBI State Finances, India Justice Report & NCRB

Key Takeaways

  • Kerala (#1, 79.0) and Uttarakhand (#2, 78.6) top India’s overall livability rankings, driven by superior healthcare, literacy, and social safety indicators.
  • Southern and Western states dominate the Top 10: Nine of the top ten states lie in South, West, or Himalayan North India, led by Tamil Nadu (#3), Goa (#4), and Karnataka (#6).
  • The Economic vs. Livability Trade-off: High-income industrial hubs like Maharashtra (#7) and Haryana (#13) score above 82 on economic output, but lose ground on urban congestion, living costs, and safety parity.
  • Eastern & Central States Lag Behind: Bihar (#25, 57.0), Jharkhand (#24, 59.5), and Assam (#21, 62.5) sit at the bottom due to human development and infrastructure deficits despite rapid GSDP growth.

Three Distinct Models of Indian Livability

Just as Visual Capitalist’s U.S. ranking highlighted sharp trade-offs between New Jersey’s high costs and West Virginia’s low scores, India’s top-performing states fall into three clear archetypes:

1. Social Development Leaders

Kerala (#1) & Mizoram (#14): Near-universal literacy, low infant mortality, and strong public healthcare lift quality of life far above what their local industrial base alone would suggest.

2. Economic & Job Engines

Tamil Nadu (#3), Karnataka (#6) & Maharashtra (#7): Powerhouses of manufacturing, IT, and startups with high wages and infrastructure, offset by metro living costs.

3. Himalayan & Coastal Havens

Uttarakhand (#2), Goa (#4) & Himachal (#5): Combine cleaner air, lower violent crime, and strong rural connectivity with steady tourism and hydro/pharma incomes.

Why High GSDP Growth Doesn’t Guarantee Top Livability

In the original U.S. analysis, affordable states like West Virginia and Arkansas ranked near the bottom because low housing costs couldn't compensate for weaker healthcare and educational outcomes. India exhibits a parallel divide.

States like Uttar Pradesh (#20) and Bihar (#25) have posted some of India’s fastest headline GDP growth rates and massive expressway expansions. However, because livability depends on per-capita outcomes—such as hospital beds per 10,000 residents, female workforce participation, and secondary school completion—states that invested early in human capital maintain a 15-to-20-point lead.
