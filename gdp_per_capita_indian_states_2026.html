Ranked: India’s Richest States by GDP per Capita (2025–26)

Just as Europe’s microstates outrank Germany on per-person output, Sikkim, Goa, and Delhi top India’s per-capita GDP rankings—while Karnataka leads all large states.

Published November 2026 · Adapted for India from Visual Capitalist’s European GDP per Capita framework · Source: MoSPI / State Directorates of Economics & Statistics (FY 2025–26 & latest revised estimates)

Key Takeaways

  • Sikkim leads all Indian states with a nominal GSDP per capita of ₹8,57,560 ($9,125)—more than 3.4 times the national average of ₹2,51,393 ($2,675).
  • The 10.1x North-South / East-West divide: Top-ranked Sikkim generates over 10 times more economic output per resident than bottom-ranked Bihar (₹84,925 / $904).
  • Large-state powerhouse: Among states with over 30 million people, Karnataka (₹4,77,033), Telangana (₹5,09,545), and Tamil Nadu (₹4,55,650) outpace larger economies like Maharashtra (₹3,94,477) and Uttar Pradesh (₹1,46,938).

1. The "Liechtenstein Effect"

Like Liechtenstein and Luxembourg in Europe, tiny populations paired with specialized industries (pharma in Sikkim, tourism in Goa) push per-capita output above ₹7.3 lakh.

2. Southern Tech & Factory Belt

All five Southern states rank in the top 13. Telangana, Karnataka, and Tamil Nadu combine high-value IT exports with dense manufacturing corridors.

3. Total GDP vs. Per-Person Reality

Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh rank #1 and #3 in total state GDP, yet rank #10 and #30 per capita because their output is shared across 125M–240M+ residents.

Why Small States and City-States Top the Chart

In Visual Capitalist’s European ranking, microstates like Liechtenstein ($230,417) and Luxembourg ($155,733) tower over Germany ($65,699) because cross-border commuters and concentrated high-value sectors divide into a small resident population. India exhibits the exact same structural pattern.

Sikkim (₹8.58 lakh) and Goa (₹7.38 lakh) lead the country by wide margins. Sikkim’s pharmaceutical manufacturing hubs and hydroelectric capacity serve a population of under 7 lakh people, while Goa’s tourism, hospitality, and coastal industrial base support roughly 16 lakh residents. Similarly, Delhi (₹5.93 lakh) and Chandigarh (₹5.45 lakh) benefit from daily commuter inflows from neighboring Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, and Punjab—mirroring Luxembourg’s cross-border workforce dynamic.

The Southern and Western Industrial Engine

When filtering out small states and union territories to look at major economies (populations above 3 crore), Telangana (₹5.10 lakh), Karnataka (₹4.77 lakh), and Tamil Nadu (₹4.56 lakh) lead India, closely followed by Haryana (₹4.41 lakh) and Gujarat (₹4.06 lakh).

Karnataka’s trajectory is powered by Bengaluru’s global capability centers (GCCs), semiconductor design, and software exports, while Tamil Nadu and Gujarat combine services with deep automotive, electronics, and petrochemical manufacturing ecosystems.

The Population Denominator in India’s Heartland

At the bottom of the table, Bihar (₹84,925 / $904) remains the only Indian state below $1,000 per capita—roughly one-third of the national average and less than one-tenth of Sikkim’s output. Neighboring Jharkhand (₹1.38 lakh) and Uttar Pradesh (₹1.47 lakh) also sit near the bottom despite massive total economic scale, underscoring how rapidly growing populations and lower industrial density dilute per-person figures.

Just as Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) narrows the living-standard gap between Poland and Spain in Europe, lower housing and food costs in states like Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, and Odisha cushion real household consumption—even as nominal income disparities remain India’s defining economic challenge.
